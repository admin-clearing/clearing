arXiv:2611.17620v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: High-level behaviors such as sycophancy and hallucination correspond to directions in a model's activation space (Chen et al., 2025). These directions, known as persona vectors, can suppress undesirable traits either at inference time, subtracting the vector during generation, or through preventative steering, adding the vector during fine-tuning, so the optimizer never needs to encode the trait in weights, then removing the vector at evaluation. Chen et al. (2025) recommend preventative steering because it preserves Massive Multitask Language Understanding (MMLU) accuracy, but validations of this recommendation have measured capability alone. We primarily evaluate results on preventative steering of sycophancy and hallucination vectors on three models, measuring three safety benchmarks alongside trait expression, coherence, and MMLU. Our findings show that preventative steering still changes refusal behavior, but by an amount that depends on the trait and does not establish that preventative persona steering is safety-neutral, and steering methods should be evaluated on several safety benchmarks.
