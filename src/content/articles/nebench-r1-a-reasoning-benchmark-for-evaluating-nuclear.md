arXiv:2611.21682v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being explored for knowledge-intensive and safety-critical applications in nuclear engineering. However, existing nuclear-domain benchmarks provide limited content coverage or focus primarily on answer accuracy without systematically evaluating the quality of the underlying reasoning. This study introduces NEBench R1, a reasoning-oriented benchmark comprising 7,546 unique multiple-choice questions extracted from U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) reactor operator licensing examinations covering 18 nuclear facilities. Each question is classified according to the NRC Knowledge and Abilities catalogs and accompanied by an official explanation serving as a reference for reasoning evaluation. Six LLMs were evaluated, with overall accuracy ranging from 34.0% to 73.9% across three distinct performance tiers. To assess reasoning quality, an LLM-based judge evaluated correctly answered responses using a multidimensional rubric. Although responses generally demonstrated strong problem comprehension, they scored lower in factual and procedural accuracy and distractor analysis, indicating that correct answers were not necessarily supported by technically sound justifications. Response clarity was also weakly indicative of technical quality. Furthermore, longer reasoning traces were associated with lower accuracy in both frontier reasoning models, suggesting that reasoning length may reflect question difficulty rather than improved problem-solving performance. Reasoning-token consumption may therefore provide an additional signal for identifying potentially unreliable responses.
