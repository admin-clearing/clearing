In the past 18 months, three regulatory signals converged that most HR technology buyers missed — because they arrived in the language of compliance memos, not product announcements.

**Signal one:** The EEOC's updated guidance on automated employment decision tools (AEDTs) now explicitly covers AI resume screening, video interview analysis, and any system that generates a ranked list of candidates — even if a human reviews the output before acting on it. Coverage is triggered by the system's role in the process, not by whether the final decision was "made by a human."

**Signal two:** The FTC has stated that "AI accuracy" in consequential decisions is a consumer protection concern, not just a product quality concern. An AI hiring tool that disproportionately disadvantages a protected class is potentially an unfair business practice under Section 5, independent of whether the employer intended to discriminate.

**Signal three:** Several city and state AI ordinances (New York City's Local Law 144 being the most operationalized) now require bias audits for AEDTs as a condition of use. The audit requirement is not one-time; it's annual.

## What 'explainable and reversible' means in practice

Regulators are not asking for XAI in the academic sense. They're asking for two narrower things:

**Explainability** means: when a candidate is screened out, can the system produce a specific, factually accurate reason that a human reviewer can verify? "The model scored them below threshold" is not an explanation. "The application did not include demonstrated experience with [job requirement X]" — with a citation — is. The distinction matters because it determines whether the adverse action reason is contestable.

**Reversibility** means: is there a defined human review process that can override the system's output, and does the system incorporate feedback from those overrides? A system whose scores can be overridden but that doesn't learn from overrides isn't "reversible" in the sense regulators mean. They want to see a feedback loop, not just an escape valve.

## What to ask your vendors

The four questions that separate compliant from non-compliant AI hiring tools in 2026:

**"What is your adverse impact testing methodology, and how often do you run it?"** Annual is the minimum; quarterly is standard in well-operated products. Vendors who answer with demographic parity only — ignoring equalized odds — are cutting corners.

**"Can your system produce a specific, citable explanation for every screening decision?"** If the answer involves the phrase "model confidence score," push harder.

**"How does your system incorporate human override feedback?"** The answer should describe a specific mechanism — fine-tuning, rules augmentation, or threshold adjustment — not a general claim about continuous improvement.

**"Has your system undergone an independent bias audit in the last 12 months?"** Ask for the report, not just a yes/no.

The vendors who can answer these questions specifically and in writing are building toward compliance. The ones who redirect to general AI safety principles probably aren't.
